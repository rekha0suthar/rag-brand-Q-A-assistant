# Tone of Voice FAQ

Answers to the questions writers ask most about our voice. The brand guidelines stay the source of truth; this FAQ adds working detail.

## How formal should we be?
Not very. We write like a friendly neighbour, not a salesperson. Contractions are fine ("you're", "it's"). Avoid corporate filler like "leverage", "solutions", or "delighted to announce".

## Can we use humour?
Yes — warm, gentle humour. Puns are welcome if they land on the first read. Never punch down, never joke about money troubles, and never use sarcasm; it reads badly in short copy.

## How many emoji?
One per social caption, maximum. None in email subject lines. None in compliance or offer text — the disclaimer always stands alone, unstyled.

## Can we use superlatives if they're true?
No. "Best", "No.1", and "guaranteed" are banned even when a stat backs them. Show the detail instead: "loved by 40,000 homes" works where "the best-loved brand" does not.

## How do we talk about festivals?
With warmth and specificity. Reference the rituals people actually do — lighting diyas, gifting sweets, cleaning the house — rather than generic "celebrate the season" lines. Diwali 2024 showed "light up" and "gifting" language resonates.

## How do we handle prices and offers?
Plainly. State the offer in one line, add the mandatory disclaimer, stop. No stacked exclamation marks, no "HURRY!", no fake urgency — compliance prohibits countdowns we can't back with real inventory or end dates.

## What about English vs. Hinglish?
Hinglish is welcome in social captions where it sounds natural ("Ghar ki feeling, sorted."). Keep product pages and emails in plain English for clarity.

## Words we reach for
"Home", "cozy", "sparkle", "little joys", "refresh", "warm", "gifting", "calm".

## Words we avoid
Superlatives (banned), "cheap" (say "easy on the pocket"), "luxury" (we're accessible, not premium), "limited stock" (unless inventory confirms), and anything that promises outcomes ("transform your life").

## Who settles disagreements?
The campaign lead, with the brand guidelines open. If the dispute touches a claim or an offer, it goes to compliance review instead of being argued in the document comments.
