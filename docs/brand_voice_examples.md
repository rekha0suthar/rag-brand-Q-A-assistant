# Brand Voice: Before and After Examples

The brand guidelines say what the voice is; this document shows it. Each example pairs a rejected line with the approved rewrite and the reason. Use these as calibration when writing or reviewing copy.

## Product Copy

**Rejected:** "The best diyas money can buy — guaranteed to impress!"
**Approved:** "Six little lights, one warm welcome."
Why: two banned superlatives ("best", "guaranteed"), an exclamation mark doing the work the words should do, and nothing specific about the product.

**Rejected:** "Luxury candles at a cheap price."
**Approved:** "Three small flames, easy on the pocket."
Why: "luxury" and "cheap" are both off-limits — we are accessible, not premium, and we never talk down our own prices.

**Rejected:** "This candle will melt your stress away after a long day."
**Approved:** "Fills the room with a warm chai scent."
Why: wellness outcome claim. We describe the room, never the customer's mental state.

**Rejected:** "Eco-friendly cushion covers for the conscious home."
**Approved:** "100% cotton covers in sun-washed colours."
Why: no environmental claims without an approved certification — and we hold none. The verified material fact is the better line anyway.

## Offer Copy

**Rejected:** "HURRY! Mega sale ends TONIGHT! Only 2 left!!"
**Approved:** "Flat 10% off above ₹999, until 2 November. Limited-time offer; terms apply."
Why: manufactured urgency, unverifiable scarcity, stacked exclamation marks, and a missing disclaimer. State the offer, add the disclaimer, stop.

**Rejected:** "Free gift wrap with every order!"
**Approved:** "Gift-ready, always — wrap and note included."
Why: "free" claims are banned when a purchase is required; gift wrap is part of the product promise, not a promotion.

## Social Captions

**Rejected:** "We are delighted to announce the launch of our new monsoon collection. 🎉🌧️✨"
**Approved:** "Chai weather. Candle weather. 🌧️"
Why: corporate filler ("delighted to announce"), three emoji against a budget of one, and no feeling.

**Rejected:** "Diwali ka sabse bada sale!"
**Approved:** "Ghar ki roshni, thodi aur."
Why: "sabse bada" is a superlative in Hinglish clothing — the banned-words rule applies in every language we write.

## Email Subjects

**Rejected:** "✨ Don't miss the BIGGEST festive deals ✨"
**Approved:** "Diwali gifting, sorted by Sunday."
Why: emoji in a subject line, a superlative, and urgency framing. Benefit-led and plain wins — "Your balcony called. It wants cushions." set the standard in Summer 2025.

## Support Replies

**Rejected:** "Your complaint has been registered and will be processed as per policy."
**Approved:** "That shouldn't have happened — send us a photo and we'll get a replacement moving today."
Why: support speaks in the same voice as marketing. A friendly neighbour does not say "processed as per policy".

## The Pattern
Across every example the fix is the same three moves: cut the banned word, swap the abstract boast for a specific detail, and say it the way a person would say it across a fence. When a line fails review, apply those three moves before escalating a disagreement to the campaign lead.
